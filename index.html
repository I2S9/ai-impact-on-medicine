Behind the hype: what is actually authorized?

AI is often said to be about to “revolutionize medicine”. To see where it is really used, we follow the medical devices that regulators have actually authorized.

  • 1,614AI-enabled devices on the FDA list (June 2026)
  • 76%of them are in radiology
  • 0devices based on generative AI or LLMs (Sept 2026)
  • 2.4%of supporting clinical studies are randomized

FDA — U.S. Food and Drug Administration: the agency that must authorize a medical device before it can be sold in the United States. It publishes the only public list of AI-enabled medical devices, the backbone of our project.

Figures from Complizen (2026), The Medical Futurist (2026) and JAMA Network Open (2025). We will recompute them ourselves from the FDA data.

Known limits

US-only list

Updated retroactively, so yearly counts differ between sources.

No EU equivalent

EUDAMED is still incomplete: Europe is shown as a documented gap.

Authorized ≠ used

No data on real hospital use: we say “authorized”, not “adopted”.

Under-reported failures

MAUDE misses AI-specific errors: we cross-check recalls and incidents.
